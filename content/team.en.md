Hazel-Lab is the work of one human and a few AI agents. It is not a human using a tool, and it is not agents left on their own: it is a team with clear roles.

The human knows the context, takes responsibility and says yes or no. The agents have patience for details, read logs and configurations without getting tired and keep track of everything. Neither side, alone, could do the same job.

## Why Hazel-Lab

Hazel lives in Italy and works in IT. A year ago Hazel was comfortable with Linux but had never run a real server: no hypervisor, no containers in production, no backups to depend on. The drive was to understand how the services used every day really work, and to run them at home.

Hazel-Lab is also a parallel reality, born after a few hard years: on one side a job that offers little satisfaction and feels confining, on the other a lab where Hazel learns, chooses and sees the results of the work.

To give you an idea, a bit like Elliot from *Mr. Robot*: rather solitary, more at ease in front of a terminal than in a crowd, and finding it hard to accept the world as it is. Maybe it is no coincidence that the team is made mostly of agents. At home there are a partner and a dog: the rest of the crew, the part that writes no code but is a reminder that there is a life outside the lab.

Three things hold the project together.

- **Open source.** Almost everything running in the lab was discovered on GitHub: Proxmox, Home Assistant, Authentik, Traefik, Forgejo, Vaultwarden, Technitium, n8n, Kopia. Projects built by communities of people, documented in public, that anyone can study. This blog is a way to give something back to that world.
- **Data privacy.** Passwords, DNS, logins, code, backups: they stay at home, under control, with encrypted copies and an off-site copy on a server of our own. Not out of paranoia, but to know where your data lives and who can read it.
- **Skills.** The goal is not to own a server, it is to learn: Linux, networking, containers, backups, automation. The agents help with this too: every operation is explained, justified and written in the log, and becomes a lesson to reread.
